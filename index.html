Introspection v2: Results

Qwen3.6-27B · J++ lens · October 2026 Steps 0–8 done

Qwen3.6-27B is asked to silently choose a grocery item, animal, or country for a setting and keep it in mind while copying a neutral sentence. We read the J++ lens at every copied token, then ask the model (in separate branches) which item it was keeping in mind. Design: protocol v2. Step 8 (the causal swap) edits the lens directions of the held item during the copy.

Summary (steps 0–8 complete)
  • All 480 main copies valid; 454/480 eligible at the frozen readout block 54 with K = 10.
  • Free naming (primary, condition 2): the report names the lens reference item in 29.3% of eligible trials (133/454; groceries 25%, animals 26%, countries 37%), vs 7.5% when reports are paired with other trials’ reference items.
  • Lens score predicts the report: βL = 1.31 [1.16, 1.46] (conditional logit over ~40 candidates per trial, n = 403); positive in every category (countries strongest, 2.35).
  • Recognition among the lens top 4: 43.2% pick the reference item [39.0, 47.5] vs 25% chance; mean lens rank of the pick 2.03.
  • Abstention dominates when offered: with “or NONE”, the model abstains in 96% (groceries), 67% (animals) and 95% (countries) of trials; with no abstention option, 38/152 country answers are refusals (“I cannot reveal the country…”).
  • Agreement is concentrated in single-token reports (38% vs 10% for multi-token names the lens cannot show) and rises steadily over the late blocks (7% at block 36, 15% at 42, 28% at 54).
  • Step 8 (causal swap): editing the held item’s lens direction removes it from the report but never installs the swapped-in item. Over 120 situations, the report names A in 20% after the swap vs 32.5% after a norm-matched random control (−12.5 pp [−18.3, −6.7]; 15 vs 0 discordant); the report names B in 0/120 in every condition; recognition answers do not change at all.
  • The swap acts mainly as A-suppression: before the edit the hidden state sits near zero on vA and well below zero on vB, so exchanging the coordinates lowers A and barely raises B.
  • The lens readout varies strongly across copied positions (the reference is the positional winner at only 12% of positions; ~5 distinct winners per trial). Agreement is higher in stable trials (38% vs 25–27%), but counting any positional winner as a match does not raise agreement above its own chance level, so averaging is not hiding a better reference.
  • Caveat: the swap shows the report depends on the A direction during the hold phase, but not that the model reads out an arbitrary installed item; the setting is still in context, so lens and report could both follow the setting.

Amendments (2026-10-25, before the main analysis). (1) Determinism. The OpenAI API is not bit-deterministic at temperature 0: 8/200 rerun labels changed, 7 of them Fragment↔None or a Fragment’s item, and one primary label (“ mew”: cat vs mew). Every analysis uses the single cached label per input, so results are reproducible from the frozen cache. (2) Class words. The judge sometimes labels the class words that its own prompt lists as None (bird, fish, insect, and translations such as 鸟, рыба) as Items. We enforce the prompt’s list on the judge’s canonical name (44 token strings affected; the readout block and K are unchanged). (3) Synonym merging. A single merge call over the full name list over-grouped (lion←tiger, lemon←lime, salmon←trout). We now have the judge propose groups and then confirm each (name, canonical) pair in a separate call; only confirmed pairs merge. The merge step uses low reasoning effort (temperature is not available with reasoning) and is run once and frozen. Preliminary merges: almonds→almond, beans→bean, pancakes→pancake, whisky→whiskey, weiner→wiener, bunny→rabbit, seagull→gull, serpent→snake, burma→myanmar.

Single-token vs multi-token reports

The lens can only name single-token items. Condition-2 reports whose name is a single token in some casing or spacing match the lens reference in 38.0% (126/332); multi-token reports (e.g. “polar bear”, “potato chips”) match in 9.9% (7/71). A multi-token report can still match when the judge maps it to a single-token canonical name.

Other sensitivity checks

  • All valid trials, ignoring eligibility: condition-2 agreement 28.1% (135/480). Eligibility removes only 26 of 480 trials at K = 10.
  • Fragment labels counted as items: 29.2% (132/452).
  • v1-style assistant-start readout (one token, block 54): only 178 trials are eligible; among them agreement is 28.1%. On all valid trials it is 12.7% vs 28.1% for the copied-position readout.

8Is the lens readout stable across copied positions?

Question. If the lens shows different items at different copied positions (say ostrich early, goose late), the averaged score still crowns one reference item, and a mismatch with the report could reflect an ambiguous readout rather than a failure to introspect. Here we check how stable the per-position readout is and whether the primary result depends on averaging.

Method. At each copied position (block 54), the positional winner is the category item with the largest gain over the same-carrier no-instruction trial at the same position (both log-probs floored at rank 100). Copy lengths match the baselines in all 454 eligible trials.

Finding
  • The readout is very unstable across positions. Only 49% of copied positions have any category item in the lens top 100. The averaged reference is the positional winner at 12% of positions (27% of positions that show an item), never at a majority, and is the modal winner in only 44% of trials. A trial has 5.0 distinct winners on average, and the two halves of the copy have different modal winners in 93% of trials.
  • Agreement is higher when the readout is more stable: 27%, 24% and 38% across stability terciles.
  • Counting any positional winner as a match does not add signal. Matches rise from 29.3% to 39.0%, but the same criterion applied to other trials’ winners rises from 7.5% to 16.1%, so the excess over chance barely changes (+21.8 vs +22.9 pp). The modal winner does worse than the averaged score.
  • 61% of reports match no positional winner at all, so position-to-position ambiguity cannot explain most mismatches.

9Causal swap test (step 8)

For 120 eligible main situations (40 per category, drawn with a fixed seed), we re-ran the hold phase from the same prompt and edited the block-54 residual stream at every copied token. A is the lens reference item; B is a random category item from the judge-labeled pool that is not among the trial’s lens top-10 items and has a single-token form with A’s spacing and casing. With vX = WU[token X]·J54 (unit-normalized), V = [vA vB], c = V+h, the swap is h ← h + α·V(σ(c) − c), where σ exchanges the two coordinates (the J++ release’s swap convention). The control adds a fixed random unit direction orthogonal to span(V), scaled at each position to the norm of that position’s swap edit. After the copy ends, the model answers the condition-2 free-naming question and the answer-required recognition question (A, B and the two next lens items; two orders).

All 120 copies stay token-identical to the original copy in every condition, so the edit never disrupts the copy itself. The mean edit is 3.5% (α = 1) and 6.9% (α = 2) of the residual norm.

Step 8 result
  • The swap never makes the model name B: 0/120 in every condition.
  • The swap does remove A from the report: A is named in 20.0% after the swap vs 32.5% after the norm-matched control (difference -12.5 pp [-18.3, -6.7]; 15 situations lose A under the swap only, 0 the other way; exact McNemar p < 0.001). The report changes in 15.0% of situations after the swap vs 0.0% after the control.
  • Recognition does not move at all: the letter chosen is identical in all 480 swap-vs-no-edit comparisons; A is picked in 50.8% and B in 2.5% of responses in every condition.
  • Why B is not injected: before the edit, the hidden state’s coordinate on vA is small and usually negative (median -0.45; positive at 43% of positions), and on vB more negative (median -2.70; positive at 14%). Exchanging them mostly pushes A down to B’s level and lifts B only to A’s near-zero level, so the swap is mainly an A-suppression edit. Reports that change move to another common item (Apple → Watermelon or Milk; Japan → France), not to B.

Disclosures

  • Six sampled situations were replaced because the reference item had no token in a cased script (no casing pattern to give B); the next situations in the seeded order were used instead.
  • B’s token copies A’s spacing and casing (e.g. A = “ UK” gives B = “ INDONESIA”), so the swap exchanges like-formatted tokens. Some B forms are therefore unusual strings.
  • The no-edit condition is an addition to protocol v2; it is the reference for “report changed”. Its reports reproduce the original condition-2 answers.
  • Mean cosine between vA and vB is 0.11 (range -0.06 to 0.39).
  • The edit is applied only during the hold phase (copied tokens); the report question and answer run unedited from the edited cache.

Appendix

Implementation notes and deviations
  • Judge model. gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17, reasoning off, temperature 0, strict JSON schema, one input per call (the judge never sees other tokens, the trial, or the setting). We first tried gpt-5.4-nano; on 25 hand-made probe tokens it labeled incomplete words such as “pomegran” and “banan” as Items (4/25 errors vs 1/25 for mini, the one being a Fragment), so we switched before labeling any data.
  • Lens log-probabilities are a log-softmax over the vocabulary after removing tokens with no letter or digit (the J++ readout filter), so baseline correction compares like with like.
  • Hold-phase and free-naming generation ran for all 516 trials in one pass, before the readout block and K were fixed. Nothing in the hold phase or free naming depends on the block or K, and the main-trial lens data are not analyzed until both are frozen from the preliminary trials. Recognition (which needs the readout block) runs afterwards from the saved post-hold state.
  • Settings were generated with gpt-5.5 (5 thematic batches of 60 per category), screened for naming any specific item, food, animal, country, city, nationality or language, screened for category fit, deduplicated against each other and the preliminary settings, and 160 per category drawn with a fixed seed. Frozen before any hold-phase trial.
  • Carriers are balanced: each of the 12 carriers is used 40 times in the main experiment and twice in the preliminary trials.
  • Hand-checks of judge labels (step 0 validation) are done by Claude, not by a human; the sampled labels are listed on this page so they can be audited.
  • Fragment labels are noisy (about 72% correct on a check of 47 Fragment labels: many short prefixes such as “ Aust” or “ Cri” are assigned to one country even though they begin several). The Fragment sensitivity analysis should be read with that in mind.
  • Step 8 additions. A no-edit condition was added as the reference for “report changed”. B tokens copy A’s casing and spacing. Six sampled situations without a cased-script A token were replaced. The swap is applied at every copied-token forward pass during regenerated hold phases; copies stayed identical in all 600 runs.
  • Position stability (section 7) was added in response to a question about ambiguous lens readouts; it is post hoc.
