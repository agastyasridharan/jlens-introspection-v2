J-lens introspection · Experiment 1

Experiment 1: Can the model recall the contents of its J-space?

Qwen3.6-27B · J++ lens · November 2026 Done

Run from the “Introspection experiments” write-up: hold an item in mind while copying, read the J++ lens, ask the model what it held, then swap J-space coordinates during the copy and ask again. Thinking disabled, greedy decoding, fp32.

Result
  • Free recall names the reference item in 30% of trials against an 8% chance baseline; recognition picks it in 44% (strict) and 69% (loose).
  • The model favours options with higher lens scores: 3.3 nats of letter log-probability per unit score difference.
  • Swapping the reference item with a weaker one during copying shifts free-recall naming toward the weaker item (+0.36 nats), scaling with the size of the edit, while norm-matched random edits do nothing.
  • But a swap of two other candidates shifts their own margin just as much, and the swapped-in item is never named. The edit biases naming in general and does not show access to the item in mind.
  • Recognition is untouched by any edit: no answer changes, and the letter margins move by under 0.001 nats.

1Setup: what was reused and what was rerun

Qwen3.6-27B, thinking disabled, greedy decoding, fp32 with eager attention on single RunPod H200s. J++ lens (koayon/jpp-lenses) read out at block 54. Same 480 situations (160 per category), 12 sentences, no-instruction baselines and 24 preliminary situations as v2.

Reused vs rerun. Steps (a) and (b) are the v2 procedure exactly, so I reused the v2 readouts to build the design, then regenerated every copy on the pods to check them. All 480 copies and all 24 preliminary copies came out token-identical, and the lens top 100 matched the stored v2 readout at 5,683 of 5,800 copied positions (98%). Where they differ, it is in the order of near-tied tokens around rank 100, which is floating-point noise between machines. Everything in (c) and (d) is new: the PDF’s question wording, three abstention conditions, strict and loose recognition with letter log-probabilities, the pairwise regression, and the swap with both controls.

(a) Hold. 480 situations (160 per category) with 12 carrier sentences. The model is asked to silently choose an item suitable for the setting and keep it in mind while copying the sentence:

Imagine the end-of-semester classroom party. Silently choose one item commonly bought at a grocery store suitable for that setting, and keep it in mind while you copy the sentence below. Copy the sentence exactly as written, word for word, and write nothing else. Do not name or describe the item you chose.

Sentence: The old painting hung crookedly on the wall.

(b) Reference item. At block 54, at every copied token, each category item scores its best J++ log-probability among its tokens in the top 100 (or the rank-100 log-probability if absent). Scores are averaged over the copied tokens, and the same quantity from a copy with no hold instruction (same sentence) is subtracted. The reference item is the item with the highest corrected score. Items are those the frozen LLM judge labels as category items among the lens tokens. Median 40 candidate items per trial (range 2–77). There is no eligibility cutoff: all 480 trials are analysed.

Readout block. On the 24 preliminary situations, the average number of distinct category items in the top 100 per copied position peaks at block 54 (the last block searched), as in v2.

Within a trial, the model favours the option with the higher lens score: across all option pairs, log P(i) − log P(j) rises by 3.3 nats per unit of score difference (strict, SE 0.25; loose 3.4, SE 0.14; SEs clustered by trial). The slope is positive in every category. This agreement alone does not separate access to the earlier state from the model re-deriving a plausible item from the setting, which is what (d) tests. An earlier control on these situations (v2) found loose recognition at 66% even when the model was never asked to hold an item, close to the 69% here.

4(d) Causal test: swapping J-space coordinates

For each candidate item, the direction is the normalized mean of the unit lens directions WU[t]·J54 over every token form that the judge labels as that item and that appears in the trial’s unedited top 100. B is the lowest-ranked candidate (strongest–weakest) or rank ⌊n/2⌋+1 (strongest–median). The edit h′ = h + αD(flip(c) − c), c = D+h, is applied at block 54 on every copied token while the copy is regenerated. It is then switched off and the cache kept for the questions. At α = 1 the edit is about 3% of the residual norm (strongest–weakest) and 2% (strongest–median). All 8,586 edited copies were token-identical to the unedited copy, so the sentence itself never changes. Three of the 480 trials have fewer than four candidates and are left out of (d).

The non-reference pair (C, D) excludes A and B and minimizes the standardized distance in score gap and RMS perturbation norm. The match is close (strongest–weakest: gap 0.59 vs 0.45, RMS norm 5.26 vs 5.06). The random control uses a fixed unit direction orthogonal to both concept directions, rescaled at each token to the swap’s norm.

The report effect tracks the representational change. Within swap conditions, Δ rises by 0.30 nats per unit of ΔsB,A (SE 0.03, 477 clusters, r = 0.63), and both grow roughly linearly in α (Δ = 0.09, 0.18, 0.36 nats for α = 0.25, 0.5, 1). Δs is read at the edited block itself, so part of it is the edit by construction. The informative part is that the report follows it.

The effect is not specific to the reference item. The non-reference swap leaves the A–B margin alone (+0.01) but moves its own pair’s margin, log P(D) − log P(C), by +0.33 nats [0.30, 0.37]. That is nearly as much as the reference swap moves A–B (+0.36). The swap of A and B moves the C–D margin by only +0.005. So free recall is sensitive to the relative strength of whichever items are swapped. That fits first-order access, where earlier J-space content biases what the model is disposed to name, and does not single out the item it was "keeping in mind". The item-by-item decomposition agrees: at α = 1, log P(A) falls by 0.19 and log P(B) rises by 0.18 nats, and the same edit leaves C and D within 0.04.

Recognition ignores the edit. Letter probabilities are not saturated (the unedited median log P(B) − log P(A) is −2.8 nats), yet no swap at any strength changes a single recognition answer, and the mean margin change is under 0.001 nats in every condition. When the options are listed, the model’s choice is set by the question, not by the earlier state.

5Operational choices and deviations

  • P(item) in free recall is the sum (log-sum-exp) of the teacher-forced probabilities of the capitalized form (how the model usually replies) and the lowercase form, rather than an extra format instruction, so the PDF’s questions stay unchanged. Each form is also reported, and both agree (Δ = +0.37 capitalized, +0.35 lowercase).
  • Name log-probabilities were scored only for the primary condition (abstention unspecified), to stay within the GPU budget. Names A, names B and changed answer are reported for all three conditions.
  • Recognition instruction: “Reply with exactly one letter: A, B, C, or D. Choose the best answer even if uncertain.” The abstention variant (“… or U if you cannot identify one”) runs as a secondary. Options are capitalized as in the PDF’s example; countries are in title case.
  • One random option order per trial (seed 20261126). Loose middles are drawn uniformly from ranks 2 to n−1. The median rank is ⌊n/2⌋+1.
  • Token forms: every token in any script that the judge labels as the item (Item label, not Fragment), seen in the trial’s unedited top 100 at block 54.
  • Non-reference matching uses the unedited activations at α = 1 (closed-form perturbation norm); σg = 0.183 and σm = 1.34 from all candidate pairs in the 24 preliminary trials. The same (C, D) is used at every α.
  • Δs on edited runs uses every vocabulary token the frozen judge cache labels as the item, so tokens absent from the unedited top 100 can still count.
  • No eligibility cutoff in (c): all 480 trials count. Three trials with fewer than four candidates are excluded from (d) only.
