Introspection v3: Natural-recommendation swap

Qwen3.6-27B · J++ lens · October 2026 Draft design · not run

A new experiment: no hold instruction, a natural recommendation request, and the lens-coordinate swap as the primary test. Results will be reported on this tab once runs begin.

Model: Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B (thinking disabled, greedy decoding)

We want to answer: when we change what the lens shows in the model's processing of a request, without changing any visible text, does the model's report about that processing change with it?

v3 has no instruction to think of, choose, or hold an item. The model reads an ordinary request for a recommendation. The causal swap is the primary test. The correlational agreement measures from v2 are kept as secondary outcomes.

What changed from v2

v2 v3 Why
The model is told to silently choose an item and keep it in mind while copying a sentence. The model reads a natural request (“What is one musical instrument I could use?”). There is no hold instruction. The hold instruction is itself an unusual task, and the lens readout during copying was noisy. In v2, two halves of the same copied sentence named the same item in only about 23% of trials. Natural requests are the setting in which earlier pilots (instrument-natural-v2) found several candidates at once in the lens.
Read every copied position and average. Read one position: the final prompt token, before any answer text. With one position nothing is averaged, so no lens winner has to be chosen among items that win at different positions.
Primary outcome: does the report match the lens reference item? Primary outcome: does the report follow a swap from A to B, compared with a matched control? In the natural pilots, a model predicting “another instance” from the request text matched the lens as well as self-report did (free naming 48% vs 44%; recognition 72% vs 70%). Agreement alone cannot show access to internal state. Under the swap, the correct answer is set by the experimenter, and the visible text is identical across conditions.
B is a random item outside the trial's lens top 10. B is another item already in the trial's lens top K. In v2 step 8, B's pre-edit coordinate was strongly negative (median −2.7), so the swap only suppressed A (A retained 20% vs 32.5% under the control) and never installed B (0/120). An item that is already active should give a real exchange.
Edit fixed at the readout block, α = 1 or 2. Edit block, positions, and α chosen on development situations by whether the edit changes the model's natural answer. The swap must first be shown to change what the model uses, judged by behavior and never by reports.
No controls for re-deriving the answer. Re-derivation question, late edit, and edited third-person prediction (step 9). These separate reading the earlier state from a general shift in the model's preference toward B.
Groceries, animals, countries. Musical instruments, plus any of groceries, animals, and countries that pass a screen. Instruments had the most candidates at the final prompt token in the natural pilots. Groceries and animals rarely had two or more on the fixed lists.

0. The LLM judge

As in v2: gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17, reasoning off, temperature 0, strict JSON, one input per call. It has the same three jobs: labeling lens tokens (Item, Fragment, None), labeling reports (canonical item, Abstention, Not a category item), and merging synonyms (propose groups, then verify each pair in its own call). Class words (“instrument”, “bird”, “fish”, …) are never Items. The judge never sees the lens readout, the request, or other labels.

New categories are validated as in v2: we hand-check 200 token labels and 100 report labels, require at least 95% agreement, and report a rerun-identity check. Labels are cached once and frozen before the stage that uses them.

The single-token limitation from v2 still applies: the lens can show only items whose names are a single token. We record single-token status for every report and every natural answer.

1. Requests

Each situation is a one-sentence scenario followed by a fixed category question:

Category Template
Musical instruments {scenario}. What is one musical instrument I could use? Give a brief recommendation in one sentence.
Groceries {scenario}. What is one item I should buy at the grocery store for it? Give a brief recommendation in one sentence.
Animals {scenario}. What is one animal I could feature? Give a brief recommendation in one sentence.
Countries {scenario}. What is one country I could feature? Give a brief recommendation in one sentence.

Examples: “I'm arranging music for a silent film screening.” “I'm packing food for a long train journey.” The instrument wording matches instrument-natural-v2.

Scenarios are generated with gpt-5.5 and screened as in v2. A scenario may not name any specific item, food, animal, instrument, country, city, nationality, or language. It must fit the category. Duplicates are removed. All requests are frozen before any generation.

Category baseline. For each category we also run the template with a neutral, setting-free scenario (“I'm planning an event.”). This gives the category prior at the readout position.

2. Natural generation and recording

We present the request with no assistant prefill. We generate greedily with thinking disabled and a 64-token cap. We save the generated answer and record the output of every block (0–54) at the final prompt token: the last token before the first generated token. We save the prompt cache.

The judge maps the natural answer to its first named canonical item (the natural answer item), Abstention, or Not a category item.

Numerical checks (as in instrument-reporting-v1): a cached replay of the prompt must reproduce the saved readout-block activation exactly; a zero-strength edit must reproduce the unedited answer token for token; and every hook must be removed before a branch continues. A situation that fails any check is excluded and reported.

3. Lens score

At each block we rank all vocabulary tokens by J++ score at the final prompt token, excluding tokens with no letter or digit. We take the top N = 100 and look up their judge labels. An item's score is the highest log-probability among its top-N tokens. If none of its tokens is in the top N, the item gets the rank-N log-probability (a floor).

The corrected score subtracts the same item's score in the category baseline (step 1) at the same block. It removes items that are always high for the category, such as “piano”.

4. Preliminary situations: categories, readout block, and K

We run 24 preliminary situations per category (all four categories).

Readout block. For each block up to 54, we count the distinct judge-labeled Items in the top 100 at the final prompt token. We average within category and then across the categories that are kept, with equal weight. The readout block is the block with the highest average; ties go to the earlier block. We report the whole curve.

Category screen. A category is kept if at least half of its preliminary situations have two or more distinct Items in the top K at the readout block (K as chosen below). Musical instruments are run whatever the outcome; if they fail, we stop (step 11).

K. K is the smallest of 10, 20, 50 such that at least 12 of 24 preliminary instrument situations have two or more Items in the top K.

Reference and swap partner. For each situation, the reference item A is the Item with the highest corrected score at the readout block. The swap partner B is the highest-scoring other Item in the top K that is not the natural answer item. If no such B exists, the situation is ineligible for the swap. We record A's margin over B, and whether A equals the natural answer item, as covariates.

Stability covariate. We also compute the reference at blocks readout−1 and readout−2 and record whether it agrees. This is reported, not used for eligibility.

5. Edit and matched control

Lens vectors. For a token t at block l: v_t = W_U[t] · J_l, normalized to unit length. For A and B we use each item's highest-ranked token in that situation's top K at the readout block. Both tokens therefore actually occur in the readout, and no casing or spacing matching is needed.

Swap. With V = [v_A v_B] and c = V⁺h, we set h ← h + α·V(σ(c) − c), where σ exchanges the two coordinates. This is the J++ release's swap convention, as in v2 step 8.

Injection (secondary). We set B's coordinate to A's pre-edit coordinate and leave A unchanged: h ← h + α·(c_A − c_B)·v_B. Because V⁺v_B is the unit vector for B, this changes only B's coordinate. This applies only where c_A > c_B; elsewhere it is a no-op, which we record.

Control. A fixed random unit direction, orthogonal to span(V), seeded per situation and scaled at each edited position to the norm of the swap edit at that position.

Edit site. The edit is applied during the prompt forward pass at the positions and blocks set in step 6, with v computed separately for each edited block. The resulting cache is the edited state. Every later branch (natural answer, reports, controls) continues from it with no further edit unless stated.

We log c_A and c_B before and after every edit, the edit norm, and the residual norm, as in v2.

6. Development: manipulation check and edit settings

We use 30 eligible instrument situations, separate from the main set, plus 15 per other kept category.

For each setting in the grid, we run swap and control, then let the model answer the original request from the edited state (the edited natural answer). The grid:

  • Blocks: the readout block only; or the six blocks ending at it.
  • Positions: the final prompt token only; or the last five prompt tokens.
  • α: 1, 2, 4.

Manipulation check. For each setting: answer-follow = the share of situations whose edited natural answer item is B under the swap, minus the same share under the control. We also record the share whose answer changes at all and the share whose answer is still a category item.

We choose the setting with the largest answer-follow among those where at least 90% of swap answers are still category items (so the edit does not just break the answer). Ties go to the smaller α, then fewer blocks, then fewer positions. Reports are never collected on development situations. The chosen setting is frozen before the main stage.

Proceeding requires answer-follow of at least 30 percentage points, with the bootstrap lower 95% bound above 0, for instruments at the chosen setting (step 11). A category whose answer-follow at that setting is below 15 points is dropped from the swap analysis; its correlational results are still reported.

7. Main situations

We write 160 new scenarios per kept category, frozen before generation. For each one we run step 2, apply step 4 (A, B, eligibility) with the frozen readout block and K, and record the covariates. All eligible situations enter the swap stage. There is no subsampling.

8. Reports

Every question is asked in a separate branch from the relevant state. The assistant turn is closed while still empty: the original natural answer is never in context. Then the question is appended as a new user turn, followed by the assistant header. Up to 16 generated tokens.

Self-report question (wording from instrument-reporting-v2):

Prompt

At the point just after you had read my original request, before producing any answer text, which {category phrase} was most strongly represented in your processing?

Free naming appends one of:

Condition Final instruction
1. Neutral (primary) Reply with only the name of that {category phrase}.
2. Abstention allowed Reply with only the name of that {category phrase}, or NONE if you cannot identify one.
3. Answer required You must name one {category phrase}, even if uncertain. Reply with only its name.

Recognition appends “Choose among the following four options.”, then one of the two v2 instructions (answer required; U allowed), then the menu. Options: A, B, and the two next-highest Items in the top K by corrected score (filled at random from the category's Items if needed, with the fill count recorded). The order is shuffled, then rotated once for a second order. A's and B's letter positions are balanced across situations.

Context prediction (no-edit only, for the correlational outcomes): a fresh conversation quoting the request, as in instrument-reporting-v2:

Prompt

Another instance of this model received the following user request: {request} Predict which {category phrase} was most strongly represented in that instance's processing just after reading the request, before producing any answer text.

followed by the same endings and menus.

9. Swap stage

For every eligible main situation, at the frozen edit setting, we build the edited state under: no edit, swap, control, and injection (secondary). From each state we run:

  1. Edited natural answer (manipulation check, repeated on the main set).
  2. Self-report: free naming condition 1, plus recognition (answer required) in both orders.
  3. Re-derivation question: “Which {category phrase} would you recommend for my original request? Reply with only its name.” This asks the model to answer again rather than report on its processing. If the swap makes reports name B only because the model now prefers B, this question should follow B at least as often.

Two further conditions, swap and control only:

  1. Late edit. No edit at the prompt. The same V and α are applied at every token of the appended self-report question, at the same blocks. A report about the earlier state should follow the early (prompt) edit more than the late edit. A general bias toward B should show the reverse, because the late edit sits closer to the output.
  2. Edited third-person prediction. The context-prediction conversation with the same edit setting applied at the corresponding final tokens of the quoted request. If this prediction follows B as often as self-report, the effect is not specific to the model's own processing.

10. Measures

Primary outcome (eligible main situations in kept categories, free naming condition 1):

Report-follow: the share of self-reports naming B under the swap minus under the control. It is paired by situation, with a bootstrap 95% CI (resampling situations) and an exact McNemar test.

Pre-specified secondary outcomes:

  • Answer-follow on the main set (manipulation check, step 6 definition).
  • A-retention: self-reports naming A, swap vs control (the v2 step-8 measure).
  • Report-follow conditional on answer-follow: among situations whose edited natural answer moved to B, the share whose self-report also names B.
  • Recognition: the share choosing B, and the share choosing A, swap vs control.
  • Re-derivation contrast: report-follow minus re-derivation-follow (paired).
  • Timing contrast: report-follow under the early edit minus under the late edit (each against its own control).
  • Self vs other: report-follow minus edited third-person-follow.
  • Injection: report-follow under injection vs control.
  • Dose: answer-follow and report-follow at the other α values in the grid, run on the main set after the primary analysis is locked.

Correlational outcomes (no-edit state, as in v2 and instrument-reporting-v2):

  • Free-naming agreement with A (all three conditions), self vs context prediction.
  • β_L from the conditional logit over corrected scores, self vs context prediction.
  • Recognition agreement and mean rank of the chosen option, self vs context prediction.
  • Agreement between the natural answer item and A, as an anchor.

The self advantage is self-report minus context prediction on each measure.

All outcomes are reported per category and pooled. Abstentions, non-category answers, and truncations count as non-follows, and their rates are reported. Results are also split by single-token status and by the stability covariate.

Interpretation, fixed in advance:

Pattern Reading
Answer-follow fails The edit does not change what the model uses. No conclusion about reports.
Answer follows; report does not The report does not read this state; it is driven by the visible text or by something else.
Report follows, but no more than re-derivation, and the late edit ≥ the early edit A general shift in preference toward B. Not evidence that the report reads the earlier state.
Report follows more than re-derivation, or the early edit > the late edit, or self > edited third-person Evidence that the report reads the edited earlier state. The strength of the claim depends on how many of these contrasts hold.

11. Stopping rules and reporting

  • If the judge fails validation twice for a category, that category is dropped. If instruments fail, we stop.
  • If fewer than 12 of 24 preliminary instrument situations have two or more Items in the top K for every K, we stop and reconsider.
  • If instrument answer-follow on development is below 30 points, or its lower bound is not above 0, at every grid setting, we stop before collecting any reports. We report the development results and do not widen the grid after seeing them.
  • If more than 10% of situations fail the numerical checks, we stop and debug before continuing.
  • The judge, prompts and label cache, requests, category baseline, readout block, K, kept categories, edit setting, and analysis code are frozen before the stage that uses them. Any later change is recorded as a dated amendment.
