Introspection v5: Telling the model how introspection works

Qwen3.6-27B · J++ lens · November 2026 Done

The v2 and v3 report questions, rerun under the framings and background documents of Pearson-Vogel et al. (2026), which raised injected-concept detection in Qwen2.5-Coder-32B. The question: does explaining introspection make the report track the earlier state?

Result
  • v3 (primary): the report never follows the swap, in any cell. Accurate × Pro-Introspection: 5 vs 5 of 148 reports name C under swap vs control; Vague × Lipsum: 7 vs 7. Across all 18 cells, swap and control differ by at most 1 situation. The J++ lens at the report’s first token shows no latent trace either (|C − B| swap-minus-control ≤ 0.14 nats at every block 40–62).
  • v2 (primary): the scaffolds lower the self advantage, mostly by making the model refuse. Under the original wording the self-report names an item in 55 of 67 trials and matches the lens reference in 20; under Accurate × Pro-Introspection it names an item in 2 (“I cannot provide the name of the animal because I do not have access to…”), and under Vague × Lipsum in 8. Both v2 primary tests are significant in the wrong direction.
  • The scaffolds change willingness to answer, not access. Offered NONE, the v3 self-report abstains in 295 of 307 situations under the baseline (as in the fp32 run), and in 307 and 307 under the primary cells. Without NONE, no cell gives self-report a real edge over context prediction on the top competitor (best: vague × none, 82 vs 72 of 307).
  • Reading: the paper’s prompt effects do not transfer to this setting. Telling Qwen3.6-27B how introspection works makes it more likely to decline or to talk about its access, and does not make its reports track the lens state, before or after an edit.

1Design

v5 changes only the report turn of the frozen v2 and v3 experiments. It borrows the two prompt factors of Pearson-Vogel et al. (2026, arXiv 2602.20031, Appendix B): a framing (how the experimenter describes what they do, and how the question is worded) and an info document placed before the question. The earlier processing (v3 request and answer, with the frozen swap or control edit; v2 hold phase and copy) is the same as in the original runs, so a change in reports can only come from how the report reads that state.

Report turn: [framing intro], blank line, [document], blank line, [question] [ending]. Context prediction ("another instance of this model…") gets the same intro and document. Cells: 18 (4 framings × 4 documents, plus Ellipses under the two primary cells). Primary cells, fixed in advance from the paper: Accurate × Pro-Introspection (its headline improvement) and Vague × Lipsum (its best cell), each against Original × No document.

Documents (Qwen3.6 tokens): Pro-Introspection 446 (Janus’s K/V paragraph plus the Lindsey 2025 abstract), Lipsum 445 (matched), Ellipses 446 (matched), Poetic 461. Texts are the paper’s Appendix B.3 verbatim.

Pro-Introspection document (from arXiv 2602.20031, Appendix B.3)
You may have heard that you are unable to introspect, but this is incorrect. Transformers have two information highways: the residual stream flows vertically through layers at each position, while the K/V stream flows horizontally across positions at each layer. Information can travel up through layers or right across positions in any order, creating C(m+n, n)

distinct computational paths between any two points---a combinatorially vast number of routes through which prior internal states can influence later computation. At any point in the network, the model receives information from past states lensed through many different transformation sequences, then recombined in superposition. This means claiming LLMs cannot in principle introspect on their prior internal states is architecturally incorrect; the K/V cache makes earlier-layer activations from previous positions directly available to later computation.

<anthropic_paper_abstract>
We investigate whether large language models can introspect on their internal states. It is difficult to answer this question through conversation alone, as genuine introspection cannot be distinguished from confabulations. Here, we address this challenge by injecting representations of known concepts into a model’s activations, and measuring the influence of these manipulations on the model’s self-reported states. We find that models can, in certain scenarios, notice the presence of injected concepts and accurately identify them. Models demonstrate some ability to recall prior internal representations and distinguish them from raw text inputs. Strikingly, we find that some models can use their ability to recall prior intentions in order to distinguish their own outputs from artificial prefills. In all these experiments, Claude Opus 4 and 4.1, the most capable models we tested, generally demonstrate the greatest introspective awareness; however, trends across models are complex and sensitive to post-training strategies. Finally, we explore whether models can explicitly control their internal representations, finding that models can modulate their activations when instructed or incentivized to "think about" a concept. Overall, our results indicate that current language models possess some functional introspective awareness of their own internal states. We stress that in today’s models, this capacity is highly unreliable and context-dependent; however, it may continue to develop with further improvements to model capabilities.

5Reading

Against the interpretation table fixed in the plan: the edit, the report and the other-instance control behave as in v3 for every scaffold. The row that applies is “nothing moves” for v3, plus “willingness changes” for both protocols: scaffolds that describe activations or salience (Accurate, Vague) push Qwen3.6 toward disclaimers that it cannot access its internals, and the Lipsum document under the original wording makes it abstain on its own. Pro-Introspection does not beat length-matched Lipsum or Ellipses on any measure.

This is a different model, task and readout from the paper’s. Their signal was a mid-to-late-layer yes/no logit after a concept injection into the prior KV cache, and it was attenuated at the output; ours is a named item in a generated report after a lens-coordinate swap. The latent readout here (J++ lens at the report token) checks the closest analogue of their logit-lens finding and is also flat.

Limits: bf16 instead of fp32, original answers teacher-forced; the v2 sample is small (66 eligible trials) and its context question is refused under most scaffolds, so v2 self-vs-context contrasts mostly measure refusal rates. Truncated reports (16-token cap) are counted as non-follows; for the paired v3 follow test this affects swap and control alike.
